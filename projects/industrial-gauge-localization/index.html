Overview

Analog pressure gauges are everywhere in industry, but reading them automatically starts with finding them. This project builds that first stage end to end: recordings became a hand-annotated dataset, a detector was trained and exported, and the result was tested on video it had never seen.

The detector is stage one of a five-stage plan (detection, rectification, needle localisation, reading, robustness). The later stages are prepared in the repository (corner annotations and classical image-processing experiments) but not built yet.

Approach

  1. 1

    Record and extract frames

    435 frames (1920 × 1080) from three recordings: a far view among pipes, a hand-held close-up and a fixed view of a second gauge. The gauge covers 10 % to 48 % of the frame.

  2. 2

    Annotate by hand, twice

    Every frame was labeled with LabelMe 5.5.0 in 2024 and again, independently, in 2025. The first pass became the YOLO labels (one class); the second pass also exists as four corner points for perspective rectification.

  3. 3

    Train YOLOv5s

    100 epochs, batch 16, 640 px, one class; the best epoch was 61. Weights are exported to PyTorch (18.5 MB) and ONNX (36.7 MB).

  4. 4

    Evaluate twice

    On 87 validation frames, and on recordings that were never annotated or used for training.

  5. 5

    Try classical image processing

    Global, local and minimum-error thresholding, each with Canny and Sobel edges, plus perspective correction and white balance, as a baseline and as groundwork for the needle stage.

Key decisions

Detect before reading

The dial has to be cropped and straightened before the needle angle can be measured, so the detector's box and the corner annotations feed the next stage.

Two annotation passes

Annotating twice measures the noise in the labels: the mean IoU between the passes is 0.845 and 35 % of frames are below 0.8. A detector cannot be expected to reach IoU 1.0 against either of them.

A small model

YOLOv5s is enough for one object type and fast: about 15 FPS on a laptop GPU (RTX 3050) and 2 FPS on CPU.

Audit the data before trusting the score

A data audit found that the original train and validation folders overlapped, so they were discarded and a fresh 348 / 87 split was made.

Limitations, stated openly

  • The validation frames are a random split of continuous video, so near-duplicates of training frames are in validation: the scores describe the same recordings, not new cameras. The videos that were never annotated are the more honest test.
  • The detector loses the gauge when the camera moves fast and the image blurs or the gauge leaves the frame (27 % detection in the third video).
  • Only the detection stage exists: there is no needle angle and no pressure value yet.
  • The three source recordings (540 MB) are not in the repository; the frames are.

What is next

  • Rectify the dial with the four-corner annotations.
  • Locate the needle (segmentation, Hough lines or keypoints).
  • Map the needle angle to a pressure value using the scale.
  • Add blur, glare, more gauge types and several gauges per frame.
  • Validate on whole recordings that are not in the training set and report the error of the final reading.
