Overview

Every month the direct and shared costs of a group of companies arrive as Excel files from different teams. The files disagree on column names and on how a company is spelled, and they contain missing, negative or repeated values. Preparing the final dataset for a Power BI dashboard by hand is slow and easy to get wrong.

This project automates it: exploratory analysis of the six sample files, a pipeline that cleans, validates and aggregates them, an allocation of shared costs by headcount, and a reporting dataset and dashboard on top.

Approach

  1. 1

    Explore the raw files

    Structure, data types, missing values and unusual amounts of the six files, documented in a notebook.

  2. 2

    Load and clean

    Column names, company names, cost titles, allocation types and periods are standardised; row-number columns and repeated rows are removed.

  3. 3

    Validate

    Zero, negative and missing amounts and rows from another month are reported, not hidden.

  4. 4

    Aggregate

    The cleaned files of all months are joined into one table.

  5. 5

    Allocate

    Direct costs stay with their company; shared costs are split by the headcount of the same month.

  6. 6

    Export and visualise

    A final Excel report feeds a one-page Power BI dashboard (cost per company, direct versus shared, monthly trend).

Key decisions

Shared costs follow headcount

A simple, explainable rule that uses the headcount of the same month, so the split moves when the company sizes move.

Nothing disappears silently

Repeated rows are removed and counted, missing amounts are filled with 0 and reported, odd rows keep a warning.

An unknown company stays visible

A company missing from the headcount list is kept as its own entity so its direct cost is not hidden inside another company.

One standard vocabulary

The same company written three or four ways, Persian and English cost titles and different period formats are mapped to one set of names.

Test the analysis

30 pytest tests cover the cleaning, the allocation and the whole pipeline.

Limitations, stated openly

  • The input is a set of six sample files with realistic problems, not a company's real ledger, and it covers two months.
  • Negative amounts (corrections) are replaced by 0 and flagged, because the correct treatment is a business decision. Three such rules are documented as decided but are not implemented yet.

What is next
