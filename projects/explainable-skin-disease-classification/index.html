Overview

A model that labels a skin photo is of little use to a clinician unless it can show what it looked at. This thesis project classifies five facial skin conditions with a fine-tuned ResNet18 and explains every prediction with Grad-CAM heatmaps, in a Streamlit demo where you upload a photo and see the prediction, the class probabilities and the explanation.

With only 375 training images the model overfits quickly, so most of the work is about evaluation: a held-out test set, per-class results, an error gallery and a candid list of limitations. It is a research project, not a medical device.

Approach

  1. 1

    Data

    Kaggle face-skin-disease: 625 photos of five conditions (acne, actinic keratosis, basal cell carcinoma, eczema, rosacea), split into train (75 per class), validation (13) and test (37), seed 42.

  2. 2

    Model

    ResNet18 pre-trained on ImageNet, final layer replaced by a 5-way linear layer, all layers fine-tuned; 224 × 224 input.

  3. 3

    Training

    Adam, learning rate 1e-4, batch 16, 15 epochs; flip, rotation of ±7° and colour jitter as augmentation; the checkpoint with the best validation macro-F1 is kept.

  4. 4

    Explain

    Grad-CAM on the last block of layer3, with layer3 and layer4 compared.

  5. 5

    Analyse errors

    A gallery of the 37 misclassified test images and the commonest confusions.

  6. 6

    Demo

    A Streamlit app with an adjustable heatmap strength, a model card and a dataset card.

Key decisions

Transfer learning

Pre-trained features make a five-class problem feasible with a few hundred images.

Layer3 instead of layer4 for Grad-CAM

The 7 × 7 maps of layer4 are one coarse blob; the 14 × 14 maps of layer3 follow individual lesions, spots and reddened areas.

Trust the test set, not the validation set

The validation set also picks the checkpoint, so its score is optimistic; the 185 test images are the number to quote.

State what the explanation is not

Grad-CAM shows where the model looked, not exact lesion boundaries, and it does not prove the model is right.

What the analysis showed

Actinic keratosis is the hardest class

7 of 37 are called basal cell carcinoma and 5 rosacea.

A bias towards basal cell carcinoma

High recall (97 %) but low precision (71 %): the model answers BCC when unsure.

Many errors are genuinely ambiguous

Small or ambiguous lesions and close-ups of a single region.

Limitations, stated openly

  • A small dataset (375 training, 65 validation images), web-sourced photos with visible watermarks, not clinical data: the model may partly rely on image style.
  • A few duplicated images (7 groups, 2 spread across splits), and the split is by image, not by patient.
  • A single training run without a fixed seed or cross-validation, so the run-to-run variation is unknown.
  • Grad-CAM is evaluated only qualitatively.

What is next

  • Cleaner data: remove duplicates, split by patient, more actinic keratosis examples.
  • Stronger backbones (EfficientNet, ConvNeXt, ViT) and class-balanced or focal loss.
  • Fixed seeds, repeated runs and confidence intervals.
  • Probability calibration and an 'uncertain' option, which matters for medical use.
  • Quantitative evaluation of the explanations and a comparison with Grad-CAM++ and Score-CAM.
